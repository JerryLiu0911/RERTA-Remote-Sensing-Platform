# Power / minimum detectable effect size

At 80% power and alpha = 0.05 (two-sided), Fisher-z approximation.

| n | context | detectable r | detectable R^2 |
|--:|---|--:|--:|
| 48 | canopy openness, seed removal (all plots) | 0.39 | 0.16 |
| 32 | erosion targets (Buffer-Core + Oil-Palm-Core plots) | 0.48 | 0.23 |
| 12 | within a single treatment | 0.73 | 0.54 |

**Ready-to-adapt statement:**

> With n = 48 plots, the study had 80% power (alpha = 0.05) to detect a correlation of r ≈ 0.39 (R² ≈ 0.16); for the erosion targets (n = 32) the threshold rises to r ≈ 0.48 (R² ≈ 0.23). Relationships weaker than this were undetectable by construction. Because this correlation-based bound is optimistic (multivariate and random-forest models have lower power, and any true effect is further attenuated by the temporal and spatial misalignment of the data), the ceiling on the *observed* effect is at least this high — so the study can exclude only strong associations as expressed in these data, not weak associations nor the underlying relationship.
